A pyramidion was the capstone of a pyramid, the point where the monument met the sky. This one crowned the pyramid of the 12th Dynasty king **Amenemhat III** at Dahshur, a pyramid built around 1850 BCE and known today as the Black Pyramid.

## The carved faces

On its east face, turned to the sunrise, a winged sun disc spreads above a pair of eyes, with signs for "beauty" and a sun disc below. The inscription expresses the king's wish to behold the beauty of Ra as the sun rose each morning. The other faces carry texts and the names of gods. Some of the signs appear to have been erased in antiquity, and it has been suggested that the capstone was either never put in place or was damaged later, perhaps during the reign of Akhenaten, when the name of the god Amun was attacked.

## A pyramid that sank

Like other pyramids of the Middle Kingdom, the Black Pyramid was built with a core of mud brick, encased in fine limestone. It was about 75 m high, with a base about 105 m long. But it stood on low ground, only about 10 m above sea level, close to the Nile, on clay that could not bear its weight. Groundwater seeped into the structure, and there were too few relieving structures above its many underground passages and chambers.

As the pyramid began to sink while it was still being built, it crushed the chambers below. The builders added beams and mud-brick walls in a hurry, but the effort failed. The king's burial chamber, with a sarcophagus carved to imitate the enclosure wall of Djoser's Step Pyramid, was never used: Amenemhat III was eventually buried in a second pyramid at Hawara, near the Faiyum. Chambers for two of his queens beneath the pyramid were plundered in antiquity, although excavators still found objects there, including a canopic jar of Queen Aat. Today the pyramid stands as a dark mound of crumbling brick, which gave it its modern name.

## Discovery and excavation

The pyramidion was found in the rubble at Dahshur in 1900 and is now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. The pyramids of Dahshur were first excavated by Jacques de Morgan from 1892, and the German Archaeological Institute in Cairo completed the investigation of the Black Pyramid in 1983.
