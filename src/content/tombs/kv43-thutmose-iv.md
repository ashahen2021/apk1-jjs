Thutmose IV, father of Amenhotep III, was buried in a tomb whose axis bends twice at right angles, a layout typical of the mid 18th Dynasty. Only two rooms were painted: the well chamber and the antechamber, where the king appears before the gods on a golden-yellow ground.

**Howard Carter** found it in 1903, working for the American patron Theodore Davis. Robbers had been there long before. A hieratic graffito records that in year 8 of Horemheb the official Maya restored the burial. Among what remained was the body of the king's chariot, now in Cairo, and the quartzite sarcophagus, which still stands in the crypt.

The king's mummy was not here. It had been moved to the tomb of Amenhotep II (KV35), where Victor Loret found it among a cache of royal mummies in 1898.
