Pepi II, throne name **Neferkara**, became king as a young boy and ruled for decades, perhaps longer than any other monarch in history: the historian Manetho credits him with 94 years. His letter to the explorer **Harkhuf**, preserved on the walls of Harkhuf's tomb at Aswan, is one of the most human documents of the Old Kingdom.

## A child on the throne

Pepi II was a son of Pepi I and came to the throne after the short reign of his half-brother Merenra. He was probably about six years old. His mother, Queen Ankhesenmeryre II, appears to have governed for him in the first years, with her brother, the vizier Djau. An alabaster statuette in the Brooklyn Museum shows her seated with the small king on her lap, dressed and crowned as a full-grown pharaoh.

## The letter to Harkhuf

Harkhuf, governor of the south, led four expeditions to the land of Yam, beyond Egypt's southern frontier in Nubia. On the last, in Pepi II's second year, he sent word that he was bringing back a "dancing dwarf of the god", probably a pygmy from central Africa. The young king's reply, which Harkhuf proudly had carved on the front of his tomb, bursts with excitement: Harkhuf must come north at once, appoint trustworthy men to sleep beside the dwarf on the boat, and check on him ten times a night, because, in Miriam Lichtheim's translation, "My majesty desires to see this pygmy more than the gifts of the mine-land and of Punt."

## The pyramid at South Saqqara

His pyramid at South Saqqara, about 52 metres high when complete, follows the standard plan of the late Old Kingdom: a valley temple, a causeway and a mortuary temple, with smaller pyramids for his queens, among them Neith, Iput II and Wedjebten. The walls of his burial chamber are covered with the **Pyramid Texts**, the spells to help the king rise to the sky and join the gods, which the queens' pyramids also contain.

## How long did he reign?

Manetho's 94 years, and the more than 90 years of the Turin king list, make Pepi II the longest-reigning king of antiquity. The highest date firmly attested in his own time, however, is the year of the 31st cattle count, about year 62 if the counts were held every two years. Many Egyptologists therefore accept a reign of more than sixty years, while the exact figure remains uncertain.

## The end of the Old Kingdom

Over his long reign, provincial governors grew wealthy and independent, building rich tombs in their own provinces and passing their offices to their sons. When he died, his successors held the throne only briefly, and the centralised pyramid state he had inherited broke apart into the First Intermediate Period. Lower Nile floods and a drier climate around this time have also been proposed as causes. Egypt was reunited about two centuries later by [Mentuhotep II](/pharaohs/mentuhotep-ii/).
