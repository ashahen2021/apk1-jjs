Crowned as a child and overshadowed for two decades by his stepmother Hatshepsut, Thutmose III went on to become Egypt's greatest military king. His victory at **Megiddo** opened Syria-Palestine to Egyptian control.

At Karnak he built the Akh-menu, a great festival hall, and carved his Annals — a campaign record drawn from the daily army journals. His tomb in the Valley of the Kings (KV34) contains the earliest complete version of the Amduat.
