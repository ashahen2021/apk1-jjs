Ramesses III, second king of the 20th Dynasty and son of its founder Setnakhte, is often called the last great pharaoh of the New Kingdom. He fought off the migrating **Sea Peoples** in the eighth year of his reign, and his mortuary temple at [Medinet Habu](/monuments/medinet-habu/) preserves the only large-scale depiction of a naval battle from the ancient Near East.

## Wars on three fronts

Around 1200 BCE the eastern Mediterranean was in turmoil: the Hittite empire collapsed and cities from Cyprus to the Levant were destroyed. Ramesses III faced attacks from two directions. In his fifth and eleventh years he defeated Libyan tribes trying to settle in the western Delta. In his eighth year a coalition of peoples his inscriptions name as the Peleset, Tjeker, Shekelesh, Denyen and Weshesh advanced by land and sea. He met them on land on the frontier in the Levant and at sea in the mouths of the Nile, where the reliefs show Egyptian ships grappling the enemy vessels while archers shoot from the shore. The Peleset are generally identified with the Philistines of the Bible, who settled in southern Canaan in this period.

## The temple of Medinet Habu

His mortuary temple at Medinet Habu, on the west bank at Thebes, is the best preserved of its kind. Modelled on the Ramesseum of his hero [Ramesses II](/pharaohs/ramesses-ii/), whose name he imitated, it was enclosed by massive walls with a gateway built like a Syrian fortress tower. Its walls carry the records of his wars, and within its enclosure the people of western Thebes took refuge in the troubled years that followed.

## Strikes and the Great Harris Papyrus

By the later years of the reign the economy was under strain. In year 29 the craftsmen of Deir el-Medina, who cut and decorated the royal tombs, laid down their tools when their grain rations were weeks late. The *Turin Strike Papyrus* records how they marched to the temples, sat down behind them and declared "We are hungry", in the first strike known to history.

The *Great Harris Papyrus*, about 41 metres long and one of the longest papyri known, was issued in the name of Ramesses III at the start of his son's reign. It lists his enormous gifts of land, people, cattle and goods to the temples, and ends with a summary of his reign.

## The harem conspiracy

The *Judicial Papyrus of Turin* records the trial of conspirators from the royal harem, led by a secondary queen, Tiye, who plotted to put her son Pentawer on the throne. The documents did not say whether the king survived. In 2012 a CT scan of his mummy, published by Zahi Hawass and Sahar Saleem, revealed a deep cut to the throat that would have been fatal, and one of his toes appears to have been chopped off. A second mummy, found with his and known as "Unknown Man E", shared his genetic profile and may be Pentawer himself.

He was buried in tomb KV11 in the Valley of the Kings, which you can find on the [Valley of the Kings 3D map](/tombs/valley-of-the-kings-map/). His mummy was among the royal bodies hidden in the Deir el-Bahari cache.

## What we know, and what is still argued

- **Established:** a conspiracy in the royal harem tried to kill him; the trial records name the plotters, including Queen Tiye and her son Pentawer.
- **Established, since 2012:** the plot succeeded. The CT scan of his mummy shows a deep cut across the throat, made while he was alive.
- **Likely:** "Unknown Man E", a mummy buried wrapped in a goatskin, which was considered ritually impure, is Pentawer. His DNA shows a father–son relationship with Ramesses III, but the identification cannot be proven.
- **Established:** the workmen of Deir el-Medina went on strike in his 29th year, as the Turin Strike Papyrus records day by day.
- **Debated:** how far his wars were real campaigns and how far they were copied from earlier kings. His Sea Peoples reliefs are original; some of his lists of foreign conquests repeat those of Ramesses II.

## Where to see Ramesses III today

- **[Medinet Habu](/monuments/medinet-habu/)**, western Thebes: his temple, with the sea battle on the outer north wall.
- **KV11**, Valley of the Kings: his tomb, known for the painting of two harpists, which gave it the nickname "the tomb of the harpers".
- **The National Museum of Egyptian Civilization**, Cairo: his mummy, moved there with those of other kings in the procession of 2021.
