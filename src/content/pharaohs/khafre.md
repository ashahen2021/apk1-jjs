A son of **[Khufu](/pharaohs/khufu/)**, Khafre built the second pyramid on the [Giza Plateau](/monuments/giza-plateau/). It is slightly smaller than his father's, but because it stands on higher ground and keeps part of its original limestone casing near the summit, it often looks like the tallest of the three.

## The succession after Khufu

Khafre was not Khufu's immediate successor. His brother Djedefre reigned first, for a few years, and began a pyramid at Abu Rawash, north of Giza. Khafre then returned to Giza for his own monument. Greek tradition remembered him as "Chephren": Herodotus, writing some two thousand years later, claimed he reigned for 56 years and kept the temples closed like his father, a story that reflects later legend rather than Old Kingdom evidence.

## The second pyramid

The pyramid was about 143.5 metres high as built, with sides of about 215 metres. To create a level base the builders cut away bedrock on one side of the site and built up the ground on the other. Inside, two entrances on the north side lead to passages and to a burial chamber cut into the rock and roofed with limestone gables. When Giovanni Belzoni forced his way in in 1818 he found the granite sarcophagus sunk into the floor, already empty, and an Arabic graffito showing that others had entered centuries before him.

## Temples, statues and the Sphinx

His complex is the best preserved of the three at Giza: a mortuary temple against the pyramid's east face, a causeway running down the slope, and a valley temple at the edge of the desert. The valley temple's walls are sheathed in red granite and its floor is of white calcite. In 1860 Auguste Mariette found in it the famous seated statue of Khafre with the falcon of Horus spreading its wings behind his head, carved from a dark, speckled gneiss quarried far to the south in the Nubian desert. It is now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.

Beside the valley temple lies the **[Great Sphinx](/monuments/great-sphinx/)**, carved from the bedrock of the quarry that supplied the pyramid's core blocks. It is usually attributed to Khafre, because of its position beside his causeway and temple, though other attributions have been proposed. The building of these monuments depended on the organised workforce housed in the town south of the plateau; you can test how such a workforce might have raised a pyramid in the [Pyramid Builder Lab](/play/pyramid-lab/).

Khafre was succeeded, after a short and obscure interval, by his son [Menkaure](/pharaohs/menkaure/), builder of the third pyramid. See the [4th Dynasty](/dynasties/4/) for the whole family of pyramid builders.
