Taharqa was the most powerful of the Kushite pharaohs of the 25th Dynasty, whose kingdom stretched from the Mediterranean deep into modern Sudan. His reign began with prosperity and a celebrated high Nile.

## A king from Kush

The kingdom of Kush, with its religious centre at Napata beneath the sacred mountain of Jebel Barkal, had taken control of Egypt under Taharqa's father, Piye, around 728 BCE. The Kushite kings saw themselves as restorers of true Egyptian tradition and devoted worshippers of Amun. Taharqa came north from Nubia as a young man and was crowned at Memphis around 690 BCE, ruling both lands from there.

## The great flood of year 6

In his sixth year the Nile rose to an exceptional height. Stelae set up at Kawa, Coptos and Tanis record that the flood covered the land without destroying it, killed the rats and snakes, kept away locusts and brought a rich harvest, a sign of the gods' favour on the king.

## A builder in two lands

Taharqa built across his whole kingdom. At Karnak he added colonnades before the main temple; one of the ten great columns of his kiosk in the first court still stands. In Nubia he built a large temple at Kawa and temples at Jebel Barkal, including one cut into the foot of the mountain itself, where a rock pinnacle was seen as a giant royal cobra. His sculpture, with its strong, rounded faces, draws deliberately on Old and Middle Kingdom models.

## The Assyrian wars

His reign coincided with the height of the Assyrian empire. Taharqa supported the kingdoms of the Levant against it; the Bible names him as "Tirhakah, king of Ethiopia" marching out against the Assyrian king Sennacherib (2 Kings 19:9). In 674 BCE an Assyrian invasion of Egypt was beaten back. In 671 BCE King Esarhaddon returned, took Memphis and captured members of Taharqa's family, including the crown prince. Taharqa retook the city, but in 667 BCE Esarhaddon's successor Ashurbanipal drove him back to Nubia. He died around 664 BCE, and his successor Tantamani made one last attempt to hold Egypt before the Assyrians sacked Thebes.

## The pyramid at Nuri

Taharqa was buried not at El-Kurru, the cemetery of earlier Kushite kings, but at a new site, **Nuri**, across the river from Jebel Barkal. His pyramid was the largest ever built in Nubia, and his burial held more than a thousand stone shabti figures. Nuri became the royal cemetery of Kush for the next three centuries.
