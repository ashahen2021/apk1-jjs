Menkaure, a son of **[Khafre](/pharaohs/khafre/)**, built the third pyramid on the [Giza Plateau](/monuments/giza-plateau/). It covers less than a quarter of the area of the Great Pyramid, but its lower courses were cased in costly red granite from Aswan, some of which still carries its rough, undressed surface: the casing was never finished.

His temples were completed in mud brick after his death. In the valley temple, George Reisner's expedition found some of the finest sculpture of the Old Kingdom, including the pair statue of the king with his queen and a series of triads showing Menkaure with the goddess Hathor and provincial deities. Builders' graffiti and the excavated workers' town date much of the building activity at Giza to his reign.
