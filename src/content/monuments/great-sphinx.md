The Great Sphinx is the oldest known monumental sculpture in Egypt: a recumbent lion with the head of a king, about 73 m long and 20 m high, carved from the limestone of the [Giza Plateau](/monuments/giza-plateau/). It lies beside the valley temple of **[Khafre](/pharaohs/khafre/)**, at the foot of the causeway that leads up to his pyramid, and faces the rising sun.

Most Egyptologists date it to Khafre's reign, around 2500 BCE, from its position in his pyramid complex and the way its enclosure and temple were cut. In the New Kingdom it was worshipped as Horemakhet, "Horus in the Horizon", and the young Thutmose IV set up a stela between its paws recording how he cleared it of sand.
