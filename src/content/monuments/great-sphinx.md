The Great Sphinx is among the oldest known monumental sculptures in Egypt: a recumbent lion with the head of a king, about 73 m long and 20 m high, carved from the limestone of the [Giza Plateau](/monuments/giza-plateau/). It lies beside the valley temple of **[Khafre](/pharaohs/khafre/)**, at the foot of the causeway that leads up to his pyramid, and faces east.

Most Egyptologists attribute it to Khafre's reign, around 2500 BCE, from its position in his pyramid complex and the way its enclosure and temple were cut; other attributions are set out below. In the New Kingdom it was worshipped as Horemakhet, "Horus in the Horizon", and Thutmose IV set up the Dream Stela between its paws after clearing it of sand.
